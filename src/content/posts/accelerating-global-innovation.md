Every large group has an innovation function. Very few can point to a line in the
accounts that it moved. The difference is almost never the quality of the ideas.

## The separation problem

An innovation team set apart from operations produces prototypes that operations
has no capacity to adopt. The team measures itself on pilots launched; the
business measures itself on throughput and cost. Neither number is wrong, and
they never meet.

## Fund the adoption, not the pilot

The expensive part of a new technology is rarely building it — it is changing the
process around it. We budget adoption alongside development, and we treat the
operational lead as the owner of the outcome rather than a stakeholder to be
consulted.

## Standardize the boring layer

Data access, identity, deployment and monitoring should be solved once for the
group. When each pilot rebuilds that foundation, most of the budget goes into
plumbing and the interesting work never gets far.

## Kill things deliberately

A portfolio with no cancellations is not being managed. Fixed review points, with
the authority to stop, are what free the capacity for the ideas that are working.

## Measure the operating number

If a program cannot name the operational metric it intends to move, and the
size of the move, it is not ready to be funded.
