Most supply chain dashboards report what already happened. By the time a delay is
visible, the decision that could have avoided it is weeks behind you.

## Earlier beats prettier

The value of a tracking system is the time between knowing and the deadline. A
plain email that arrives four days early beats a beautiful dashboard that
confirms yesterday's problem.

## Exceptions, not status

Nobody watches a screen showing that things are fine. Systems should be silent
when the network is behaving and loud when a shipment breaks its expected
pattern.

## Alternatives decided in advance

Visibility only pays off if there is a second option ready. Alternative routings,
buffer stock positions and pre-qualified carriers are what convert a warning into
a non-event.

## One version of the truth

Carriers, freight forwarders and internal systems each hold a partial picture.
Reconciling them into a single shipment record is unglamorous integration work,
and it is the foundation everything else sits on.

## Plan for the closure

Lanes close. Ports congest. The question is not whether it happens but how many
hours it costs when it does.
